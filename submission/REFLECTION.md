The anti-pattern our team is most at risk of is treating the lakehouse as a dumping ground and skipping maintenance. LLM observability logs and agent traces arrive continuously, so small files, stale embeddings, duplicated Bronze rows, and orphan files can accumulate quietly. Queries may still run at first, but cost, latency, and trust degrade over time.

My mitigation is to make Bronze, Silver, and Gold contracts explicit, then automate the operational jobs instead of leaving them as manual cleanup. Streaming writes should enforce schema, Silver should deduplicate with clear merge rules, and hot tables should have scheduled compaction and clustering. We should also run vacuum or snapshot expiry together with orphan-file detection, and pin table versions for training or replay. The lesson from this lab is that ACID table formats are necessary, but production lakehouses also need maintenance, lifecycle policy, and provenance discipline.
