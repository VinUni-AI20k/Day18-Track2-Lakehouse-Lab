Our team is most at risk of the "small-file explosion" anti-pattern from slide §5. We tend to optimize for shipping speed and accept many tiny appends from upstream jobs, assuming storage can absorb it. In practice, this hurts query latency, increases metadata overhead, and makes performance unpredictable for dashboards. In NB2, we saw exactly this shape: many small files made selective reads slower, and OPTIMIZE + ZORDER improved the path significantly.

The reason this risk is high for us is organizational, not technical: ingestion and analytics are owned by different people, so no one "feels" the full cost immediately. To avoid repeating it, we would define file-size and compaction SLOs, schedule regular OPTIMIZE windows, and enforce partitioning/clustering rules for hot filters (for example, model, tenant, or date). We would also add simple monitoring on file counts and table history so drift is visible before performance complaints appear.
